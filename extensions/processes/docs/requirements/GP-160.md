  - On a single-component road network layer, the shortest path node returns a line between two points whose length matches the sum of the traversed edges, measured in metres.
  - The isochrone node returns one polygon per threshold around the origin, and a point outside the network extent is reported as not snapped.
  - The nearest facility node assigns each demand point to the facility closest along the network, not in straight line.
  - The run log always reports the number of connected components of the graph and the points that did not snap.

Shortest path, time and distance isochrones, service areas and assignment to the nearest facility,
on an imported road graph. It recurs in ten of the surveyed domains (mobility, logistics, emergency
health, siting of offices and stops) and was the one analysis family missing entirely. Four GeoFlow
nodes sit on one graph builder and one Dijkstra: isochrone and service area are a single-source
Dijkstra with a cost ceiling, nearest facility a multi-source one, shortest path the same stopped at
the target. Costs are geodetic (haversine), never degrees. The log always reports connected
components and unsnapped points, the two causes of almost every unexpected result. Sample data must
be a real network (one component), not display-only line layers.
