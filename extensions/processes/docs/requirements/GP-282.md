  - A car isochrone on an OpenStreetMap network never crosses footways, steps or pedestrian areas; a walking isochrone does.
  - A walking or emergency-vehicle route ignores one-way restrictions; a car route respects them.
  - When the road class attribute is missing, no edge is filtered and the run log says so.
---

The network nodes take a "Mode" field (walking, bicycle, car, emergency vehicle, custom) instead of
expert parameters. A mode decides three things: which edges are passable, whether one-way applies, and
the speed per road class, with a declared speed limit winning over the table. "Custom" keeps the
expert fields. Without it, a car isochrone crosses pedestrian areas and a walking one obeys bans that
do not exist for pedestrians: wrong answers that look right. If the class attribute is missing the
filter must not act, and the log says so, since discarding edges silently makes half a city look
unreachable. The sample network must include footways and cycleways.
